How This Works

The Data Source

NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center streams minute-by-minute measurements from spacecraft parked at the L1 Lagrange point, ~1.5 million km sunward of Earth — currently ACE and DSCOVR, with SOLAR-1 and IMAP joining as replacements. Several satellites can report for the same minute; NOAA flags exactly one reading active, and Heliosphere only ingests that flagged row, skipping minutes where none is available.

Source: NOAA SWPC — Real-Time Solar Wind

GSM Coordinates — What Bx, By, Bz Mean

The magnetic field is reported in Geocentric Solar Magnetospheric (GSM) coordinates: Bx points along the Earth–Sun line, Bz is aligned with Earth's magnetic dipole (positive = north), and By completes the right-handed set (roughly dawn–dusk). Bt is the total field magnitude, √(Bx²+By²+Bz²). This layout is chosen specifically because it puts Earth's dipole field in one plane, which is what makes southward Bz easy to read off directly.

Source: NASA SPDF — Coordinate Systems (Appendix C)

Clock Angle & Southward Bz

The clock angle θ = atan2(By, Bz) measures where the field points in the Y-Z plane: 0° is due north (Bz fully positive), 180° is due south (Bz fully negative). A southward-pointing field partially cancels Earth's own northward field at the dayside magnetopause, allowing magnetic reconnection to open a path for solar wind plasma to enter the magnetosphere. That's why sustained negative Bz — not speed or density alone — is the primary driver of geomagnetic storms.

Newell Coupling Function

Coupling = v4/3 · Bt2/3 · sin8/3(θ/2), where v is solar wind speed and Bt is the transverse (By–Bz) field. It was empirically fit across ten independent measures of magnetospheric activity (Dst, Kp, auroral power, and others) and correlates better with observed geomagnetic activity than most competing formulas. The output is a relative index, not a physical unit — read it as "how hard the solar wind is currently driving the magnetosphere," not a literal flux measurement.

Source: Newell et al. (2007), "A nearly universal solar wind-magnetosphere coupling function," JGR Space Physics

dBz/dt — Storm Onset Warning

This tracks how fast Bz has moved over the trailing 15 minutes. A sharp negative swing (Bz dropping quickly) often precedes the rapid "main phase" onset of a storm, ahead of what the instantaneous Bz value alone would suggest — it's an early-warning signal, not a severity measure.

Storm Tier — Live Heuristic vs. NOAA's Official G-Scale

The banner at the top shows both, side by side, pulled from NOAA's own feeds in real time. NOAA Official is the authoritative G-scale, derived from the Kp index — a 3-hourly measurement computed from a global network of ground magnetometers — so it only updates a few times a day. Live Estimate is a solar-wind-driven heuristic: it approximates likely severity every minute from Bz, speed, and the Newell coupling index, using the same physics that eventually shows up in Kp. When they disagree, trust NOAA Official; the heuristic exists to give you a same-minute early read while you wait for it.

Source: NOAA — Space Weather Scales (G1=Kp5 … G5=Kp9)
